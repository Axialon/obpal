ob.Pal Desktop {version} for Windows
==============================

Makes your phone this PC's mouse and keyboard, through the ob.Pal Link browser extension: in
every window (Whole PC), or only in the programs you allow.

Install
  1. Keep this folder somewhere permanent (browsers start ob.Pal Desktop from here).
  2. Double-click install.cmd. It registers the helper for Chrome, Chromium, Edge, Brave and
     Vivaldi, for your Windows user only (no administrator rights needed).
  3. In the ob.Pal Link popup, pick PC and allow the permission it asks for.
  4. Click "Control the whole PC". Or, one program at a time: bring the program to the front,
     come back to the popup and click Allow.

Update
  Switch ob.Pal Link away from PC and close its options page if it's open (or close the
  browser), then unzip the new version over this folder. There is no need to run install.cmd
  again unless you moved the folder.

On the phone
  Trackpad (the Rotate tab): drag to move the pointer, tap to click, tap again to double-click,
  hold then lift to right-click, hold then move to drag, two fingers to scroll (a flick
  carries on), pinch to zoom. The wheel along its edge scrolls too.
  Point: the phone is a mouse, held like a remote. Aim to move the pointer. Left clicks and
  Right right-clicks where they went down; press one and aim away to drag. The wheel between
  them: turn it to scroll (turned fast, it spins on), tap it to middle-click, or hold it and
  aim to scroll. Above them: zoom out, centre the pointer, zoom in. Where the phone's browser
  allows, its volume keys work too: up is Left, down holds the wheel.
  Gamepad, with Whole PC: a desktop controller that types no letters. The left stick moves
  the pointer and the right stick scrolls. A or RT clicks (held, it drags), X or LT
  right-clicks, and pressing the left stick middle-clicks. B is Esc, Y is Enter, the D-pad
  gives the arrows, LB and RB go back and forward, Menu opens Start, and View goes to the
  last app. For one program, the gamepad gives the game keys instead (the left stick is
  W A S D, A is Space, and so on).
  Keyboard: the Keyboard button in the phone's tray types into the PC with your phone's own
  keyboard, autocorrect, predictions and swipe typing included, with Esc, Tab, the arrows,
  Backspace and Enter on a row above it. While a text box on the PC has the focus, the phone
  offers Type by itself. In a password box, the phone types into a password field of its
  own, so nothing is suggested, learned or kept.

Safety
  - Nothing reaches the PC until you pick PC in ob.Pal Link and turn on Whole PC or allow a
    program. Allowed programs are listed, and can be removed, in the extension's options.
  - Ctrl+Alt+Backspace stops everything at once. So does Pause all, in the options.
  - Windows keeps programs running as administrator out of reach.
  - To offer the phone's keyboard, ob.Pal Desktop asks Windows which kind of control has the
    focus (a text box, a password box). It never reads what a field holds.

Uninstall
  Double-click uninstall.cmd. It removes the browser registration, the settings and the files
  in this folder, and the folder itself once it's empty. The browser can stay open: a running
  ob.Pal Desktop notices and stops by itself. To start over completely, also remove ob.Pal Link
  from the browser's extensions page (that clears its pairings).

Windows may warn that the program is unrecognised: it isn't code-signed yet. Source:
https://github.com/Axialon/obpal (desktop/). Questions: hello@obpal.blackboxes.net
