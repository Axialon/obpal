# F0-R: bounded physics backends and measured selection

Status: lane-measured for integration review. Both native adapters pass the unchanged fixtures in Chromium at normal CPU and 4x throttle; Custom and Rapier also pass the Node comparison. PhysX remains browser-only. The measured rule selects Rapier. F1 is not released by this document; no further playable sim has migrated.

## Selection and dependency boundary

`src/sim/physics/selection.ts::PHYSICS_SELECTION` is the only production selection object: its `id` and literal lazy `load` import select **Rapier**, as confirmed by the measured rule below. `createSelectedSimulation` validates before loading and never substitutes another engine on failure. Rapier uses native spherical impulse joints with maximal coordinates; PhysX uses actual reduced-coordinate articulations. Both meet the tighter articulation/energy tier. The custom comparator deliberately lacks box/box contacts and remains production-ineligible despite passing its narrow fixtures.

`candidates.ts` and `bench-page.ts` belong only to the benchmark. Neither is imported by production selection. No current playable, shared kit or registry imports the new selection. Importing an unselected sim therefore adds no engine request; the browser bench also checks that no engine arrives before opt-in and that no other candidate is fetched afterwards. A future adoption must use selection, not candidates. No package or lockfile is edited here: both candidates were already pinned as development tools in the request. On accepted integration, the coordinator moves **only the measured selected engine** to production dependencies, retaining the other solely for development comparison. Measured promotion is `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat@0.21.0`; do not promote both.

## Interfaces, units and bounded state

`schema.ts` defines owned SI values: metres, seconds, kg, N, Nm, radians; quaternions are xyzw and normalised/canonicalised. Bodies use finite diagonal inertias derived from sphere or box dimensions and mass, not claimed hardware tensors. Frames and anchors are local to their named bodies. The shared half-space's local normal is +Y; PhysX's native +X plane is rotated at the shape, not by changing scene coordinates.

`Simulation` validates and owns a scene, then drives one `Backend` through the existing `FixedWorld`. `read`, `force`, `torque`, `step`, `sleep`, `memoryBytes` and `dispose` form the native boundary. Only snapshots, stable scene IDs, diagnostics and explicit commands cross it; native handles never do. Input scenes, returned scenes, snapshots and metadata do not alias mutable live state. Shapes are sphere, oriented box and fixed infinite half-space. Joints form a validated forest; closed loops and fixed children are rejected.

Defaults are 64 bodies, 63 joints, 128 combined internal/external force operations, 20,000 N and 2,000 Nm aggregate per-body wrench caps, 100 m/s, 100 rad/s and 10,000 m state bounds. Callers may tighten but not increase them. The outer tick is 1/240 s, with at most 12 ticks and 0.05 s accepted per frame. Invalid frame times are counted and ignored by the preserved clock; overload time is dropped and reported, not replayed as a later burst. Joint/wheel/buoy work is reserved before accepting external commands; each queued force applies for one outer tick. Count limits bound work, **not execution time or high-speed tunnelling**.

`init` coalesces concurrent callers, retries rejected initialization and frees late completion after reset/disposal. A backend fault retains the last completed public snapshot, clears commands and rejects further stepping until explicit recovery. Re-entry disposes the failed native world; reset rebuilds initial state and IDs and clears targets. Disposal is idempotent and terminal. Even initialization plus release failure leaves an inspectable fault rather than permanent loading. Sleep/wake targets a connected articulation island. Rapier automatic sleeping is disabled so slow motion and analytic damping are not truncated; explicit sleep/wake remains supported and tested. Native sleep decisions are allowed to differ; replay compares the same engine and runtime, never cross-engine bit equality.

Rapier caches its initialized module but frees each world. PhysX retains one process-lifetime SDK/WASM cache, while each simulation owns and releases scene, dispatcher, actors/articulations and owned temporary values in reverse dependency order. The dispatcher binding exposes a destructor rather than a release method. WebIDL `[Value]` pose/velocity getters return static scratch storage: copy immediately and never destroy those returned handles. Link storage belongs to its articulation; it is not double-freed as an independent actor. Release errors are reported. This lifecycle is testable but **not a measured native leak-free certification**.

## Four implemented body behaviours

Motors apply the same bounded torque law to all candidates: saturate proportional torque, subtract relative angular-velocity damping, then saturate the servo torque again. Applying damping after proportional saturation prevents the saturated-controller oscillation found by the offline tests. Targets clamp to an elliptical swing cone and signed twist interval in joint frames, with q/-q and wrap handled canonically. A separate finite compliant stop supplies constraint reaction (stiffness 1,200 Nm/rad, damping 30 Nm s/rad, capped by the world torque budget). Servo torque and stop reaction are not confused in the saturation metric. This is **not a hard anatomical-stop guarantee**; peak and final overshoot are measured and must stay within the comparison floor. Native PhysX links are the integrator/constraint solve, but their own drive models are not compared in this stage.

Wheels are load-bearing spring/damper rays with friction-limited longitudinal drive and lateral damping, acting at contact points. The query is identical across engines and supports only static plane/sphere/box terrain. There are no native vehicle SDK claims, deforming tyres, wheel angular inertia or moving-ground coupling. A four-wheel chassis must support its weight and travel; a moving render-only wheel does not count.

Buoyancy sums submerged spherical sample fractions in a horizontal, static-density fluid. Lift is density times gravity times displaced volume; sampled velocity-dependent drag and force-at-point generate both translation and torque. Vertical downward gravity is required. Hull sample volumes are model weights, not a mesh volume estimator. Waves, fluid-fluid interaction and CFD are outside this adapter.

All fixture dimensions, masses, gains and fluid/tyre parameters are synthetic simulation defaults, not imported robot specifications. None authorises physical-device control.

## Executable like-for-like comparison

`fixtures.ts` supplies one scene and one measurement function for each candidate. Seven fixtures run twice: sphere drop/contact (4 s), two-link motor chain (8 s), saturated cone target (8 s), four-wheel support/traction (6 s), floating trim (8 s), undamped articulated energy (5 s) and damped spin (3 s). The two-link and energy fixtures specifically use native reduced coordinates in PhysX. Separate shared contract tests exercise sphere/sphere and rotated box contacts, command budgets, ownership, async rejection/race, force persistence, faults, reset and sleep/wake. Three additional shared lifecycle probes execute inside every measured candidate.

CPU p50/p95/p99 measure **the full `Simulation.advance(1/240)`**, including common actuators, native integration, validation and snapshot publication. They exclude after-step metric computation and include the custom solver's four microsteps; they are not bare native-kernel timings. Initialization and dynamic-import cost are recorded separately, with cold init combining the first fixture's initialization and factory load. Subsequent worlds intentionally reuse module caches. No warmup number is substituted for a cold number.

Penetration is maximum supported-body depth against the fixture's analytic half-spaces; it is not a full mesh/self-contact penetration measurement. Constraint error is the maximum separation of each pair of joint anchors. Motor settling requires <0.07 rad target error and <0.1 rad/s relative angular speed sustained through the last 0.5 s. Wheel load/displacement use the last one second. Buoy trim is the final centre's height relative to water. Energy drift is maximum absolute relative error of the undamped bob's mechanical energy from its initial displaced state. Damping checks both absence of energy injection and the analytic exponential envelope.

Replay samples owned state every 24 ticks and at the final tick. It checks all sampled positions, velocities, angular velocities, rotations and sleep flags with matching IDs. `repeatabilityMaxDelta` is the maximum of respective SI vector norms and quaternion angular distance; the 1e-6 threshold applies in each respective unit. It is **same-host, same-build replay**, not cross-browser/network determinism. No reference trajectories are fabricated for unrun engines.

The browser runner builds a **separate in-memory entry** with Vite (`configFile:false`, `write:false`), serves it through Playwright route fulfilment at the guarded local origin, and aborts outbound requests. No application route, production entry, persistent profile, extra port, CDN or build configuration is added. It runs fresh contexts at normal Chromium CPU and a 4x CPU-throttle profile; this is a reproducible stress proxy, **not a measured phone**. It checks JS/runtime errors, lazy isolation and emitted artifact inventory. Each candidate has a 240 s wall budget; the guard prints partial/blocked rows before reporting failure.

`transfer.rawBytes/gzipBytes` count exact emitted candidate artifact bytes fulfilled to that context and their locally computed gzip-9 sizes, including PhysX WASM. Filenames, SHA-256 and sizes accompany the total. Common harness delivery is recorded separately. These are reproducible download-payload sizes, **not observed compressed HTTP wire transfer**: request interception disables ordinary caching and bypasses real compression. Resource Timing encoded bytes remain separately nullable. Browser heap, where available, is JS used-heap accounting; PhysX `memoryBytes` is the **entire shared WASM heap capacity**, not per-world live allocations. Rapier/custom native memory is null when not exposed. Never substitute zero for unavailable memory.

## Floors and the deterministic selection rule

`fixture-validation.ts` owns physical comparison floors: <=25 mm plane penetration and anchor error, <=0.03 rad peak/final cone overshoot; drop within 20 mm of expected centre and <0.1 m/s; settled normal motor; saturated servo <=2.500001 Nm and relative speed <0.2 rad/s; wheels move >1 m and support 98.1 N within 20%; hull trim within 80 mm, speed <0.1 m/s and displacement within 0.0004 m3 of 0.003 m3; undamped energy drift <25%; damping ratio within 0.002 of analytic, without >1e-8 J energy gain. These are initial comparison floors, not the F1 5 mm foot-contact or humanoid fidelity acceptance.

`candidateFailures` rejects incomplete fixtures, lifecycle failures, missing/non-finite metrics, bad percentile ordering, failed replay and browser errors. The original failures remain visible even if another candidate passes. Nullable unsupported measurements never become successes. `decision.ts::selectEngine` requires three measured rows from one profile; custom is measured but explicitly production-ineligible. For eligible native engines: first prefer the tighter articulation/energy tier (anchor <=5 mm, peak cone <=0.03 rad, energy drift <=10%), then the lowest worst-fixture p95 CPU. Only within 10% of the fastest in the best tier does smaller measured gzip payload decide, with a stable ID tie-break.

`selectAcrossProfiles` is the single integration rule: require all six browser rows, require a candidate to pass both normal and stressed profiles, then apply the ranking in the **4x-stressed profile** among those eligible in the normal one. If a candidate was blocked or no candidate meets this rule, the result is `pending`, not a fictional winner. The current measurements confirm the existing Rapier constant, and F1 stays gated. The browser table prints both per-profile decisions and `integrationDecision`; the coordinator need not interpret conflicting per-profile winners by hand.

Custom is measured and passes, but its limited contact/articulation support still excludes it from production selection. This is a same-machine benchmark and 4x CPU stress proxy, not phone or humanoid acceptance.

Root causes and fixes:

- Rapier energy: this binding's spherical multibody joint starts at zero generalized rotation, snapping the bob from 0.5 rad to identity on the first tick. Energy drops from 0.420319847 J to 0.000000936 J immediately (99.9998%); this is initialization loss, not integrator drift. Native spherical impulse joints preserve the supplied pose and velocities. The first-tick regression now checks pose preservation independently of the five-second energy floor.
- Rapier damping: native automatic sleeping zeros the slowly decaying spin, producing ratio 0 instead of 0.014995577. Disable automatic sleep while retaining explicit sleep/wake; the measured ratio becomes 0.015110070. No forces, damping coefficients, timestep or fixture floors were changed.
- PhysX abort: `P.destroy(getGlobalPose())` attempts to delete static WebIDL `[Value]` return storage. The named abort capture says `destroy getGlobalPose return`; the installed `PxErrorCallbackImpl` emitted no SDK error before the allocator abort. Copy pose and velocity getters immediately without destroying them; destroy only constructor-owned temporary values. The installed binding also exposes the dispatcher destructor rather than the previously invented `release()` method. The adapter now typechecks against installed declarations and validates native pointers, scale and scene descriptor.
- PhysX energy after the abort fix: the articulation joint defaults to Coulomb friction 0.050000000745, absent from the shared joint law. Disabling automatic sleep/stabilization alone left 95.3089% drift. Setting joint friction to zero reduced drift to 1.0022%; sleep and stabilization defaults were restored. Motors still receive the same common bounded torque/damping law as every candidate. Zero CPU worker threads and the existing creation/release order remain sufficient; no extension-init call was needed for these exposed APIs.

Lane acceptance: all four product typechecks passed; Vitest passed 2,777 tests with 14 skips (201 files passed, one skipped); the isolated physics gate passed 11/11; the full sims suite passed 437/437 in 38.5 minutes. Both guarded browser runs reported 19 test-browser lines before and after, **no new sessions**. The masked added-line/path privacy scan found no issues.

Before/after logs, the first-tick trajectory and native abort/default-friction captures are retained under ignored `artifacts/f0r-engines/`. Canonical measurements are `after/node.json`, `after/browser-full.json` and `after/table.md`; complete acceptance logs are `after/check.log`, `after/final-bench/sims.log` and `after/full-sims/sims.log`. No evidence or machine paths are committed.
