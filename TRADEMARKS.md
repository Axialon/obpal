# DRAFT: ob.Pal name and logo policy

**Draft for maintainer review.** This is the project's proposed naming policy, not legal advice or a statement that a trademark is registered.

The code is licensed under [MIT](LICENSE). That license lets you use, change and distribute the code, including a fork. It does not license the ob.Pal name, wordmark or logo for presenting your fork, mirror or modified build as ob.Pal or as endorsed by its maintainers.

## Naming a fork

Give a public fork or modified deployment its own name and logo. Say plainly that it is based on ob.Pal, link to the [upstream source](https://github.com/Axialon/obpal), retain the MIT copyright and license notices, and identify your changes. Descriptive references to ob.Pal are welcome when they do not imply endorsement.

A separate deployment should show its own origin and the fork or mirror marker. The marker helps an honest operator tell visitors where they are; a malicious copy can remove it. It is not a certificate or proof that a build is legitimate. Visitors should check the domain and compare the connection seal on both devices, as explained on the [trust page](https://obpal.blackboxes.net/trust/).

## The upstream project

The upstream site is **https://obpal.blackboxes.net**, and the repository is [Axialon/obpal](https://github.com/Axialon/obpal). The trust page links to its Chrome Web Store listing and npm packages. A copied name, logo or seal illustration alone does not identify the upstream project.

Existing third-party asset notices still apply; see [README.md](README.md#license). Questions about using the name or logo can be sent through the contact listed in the README.
