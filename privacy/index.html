Privacy policy

Your phone talks to your screen, and nothing else is kept.

Last updated 27 September 2026

  • No accounts. Nothing to sign up for, on either device.
  • No analytics or tracking. No ad or analytics scripts, and no tracking cookies.
  • Direct and encrypted. Controller input and typing go from your phone to your screen over an encrypted connection and are never stored.
  • Your files stay yours. Models you open are read in your browser and never uploaded.

This policy covers ob.Pal at obpal.blackboxes.net: the website, the phone controller that opens in your phone's browser, the 3D viewer, the ob.Pal Link browser extension, and the ob.Pal Desktop helper for Windows.

Pairing and the connection

The QR code holds a one-time pairing secret. It sits in the part of the link that browsers never send to a server, so it stays on your two devices. The ob.Pal signaling service only sees a room identifier derived from it.

To connect, both devices briefly join that room on the signaling service, which runs on Cloudflare. They swap the technical details a WebRTC connection needs, including network addresses. The service passes these messages between the two devices in memory and doesn't store or log them.

After that, your phone and your screen talk over an encrypted WebRTC connection: directly on your own network when they can, otherwise directly over the internet. Only when neither works is it relayed through a TURN server, Cloudflare's. The relay sees the two devices' network addresses and when they're connected, as any relay must, but not what they send: that's encrypted end to end between your phone and your screen, and never stored.

The service keeps no request logs of its own. It logs only its own errors, never with a network address, a code, a secret or a room in them. To stop abuse, it counts how often a network address connects or tries a short code, in memory for a few minutes at most. A short code is kept with the address of the screen showing it while the code is live, ten minutes at most, so no one address can hold too many. Cloudflare, which hosts ob.Pal, handles network details such as IP addresses to deliver and protect the site, under its own privacy policy. None of this holds what your devices send each other.

The ob.Pal Link extension

  • Only the tab you switch on. It sends your phone's input into the tab you turned on with This tab, and nowhere else. With All sites turned on, a small bridge also loads in other tabs so control can follow the page as it navigates, but it stays idle there.
  • Doesn't read your browsing. It doesn't read page content, your browsing history or what you type on the computer. Its only job is to turn your phone's input into input on the controlled page or, with ob.Pal Desktop, on your PC.
  • Addresses stay in the browser. The popup shows which site the current tab is on. The extension also checks the sites in the controlled tab against a built-in list, to suggest control settings to your phone (flight controls for a flight game, say). The phone gets the suggestion, never the address.
  • Local storage only. It remembers the mode you picked (Controller, 3D, Keys or PC) and the look of its pages. Until the browser closes, it also keeps which tab is controlled, whether your phone is connected and, for PC, what ob.Pal Desktop reports (the program in front and your allowed programs). All of this stays in the browser.
  • Typing passes straight through. What you type on the phone's keyboard goes over the encrypted connection to the extension and on to ob.Pal Desktop, which types it. Neither keeps it. For a password field, the phone uses a password field too, so nothing is suggested, learned or kept.
  • Remembered phones stay on your devices. After you pair a phone online, the extension and the phone each keep a pairing key and the other device's connection certificate fingerprint (the extension also keeps the phone's name and when you paired it), so the phone can connect directly over your local network when the internet is down. None of it leaves the device, and you can forget a phone in the popup or in the phone's settings.
  • Talks only to ob.Pal and your phone. Its only network use is the ob.Pal service (for pairing and relay credentials) and the connection to your phone. For PC, it also talks to ob.Pal Desktop, on the same computer. It loads no remote code and has no analytics.

The ob.Pal Desktop helper

ob.Pal Desktop is an optional Windows program that lets your phone move the mouse, click, scroll and type on your PC. The extension talks to it on your computer only; it has no network access of its own. It does nothing until you pick PC. With Whole PC on, your phone's input goes to whichever window is in front, the browser included; otherwise only to the programs you have allowed, one by one, while they are in front. It can't reach programs running as administrator, and Ctrl+Alt+Backspace stops it at once.

So that your phone can offer its keyboard when you click into a text field, the helper checks about four times a second whether the control with the keyboard focus is a text or password field. It reads only what kind of control it is, never what's in it. Text you type on the phone is typed, not kept.

Your allowed programs, the Whole PC setting and a short activity log (start and stop times and counts, never what was typed) are kept in your Windows profile (%APPDATA%\obpal). You can remove programs in the extension's options, and its uninstall.cmd removes all of it.

The viewer and your files

Models you open, drop in or add from a connected folder are read and displayed in your browser and never uploaded. When you connect a folder, the browser remembers your permission on this device until you disconnect the folder.

What's stored on your device

Your theme is kept in a bb_theme cookie that all Blackboxes sites share, so every site opens in the same look. Your accent, viewer and remote preferences, and your last scene stay in your browser's local storage. None of this is used for tracking, and clearing your browser data removes it.

Sponsoring and donations

Payments are handled by Stripe on its own checkout pages, and ob.Pal never sees your card details. For the public supporters list, ob.Pal records the amount and the time. Your name is recorded only if you choose to show it; otherwise the list says "Anonymous".

Who else is involved

Two services handle data for ob.Pal:

  • Cloudflare: hosting, signaling, relays, and email to the address below.
  • Stripe: payments.

ob.Pal doesn't sell or share data with anyone else, and doesn't use it for advertising.
